Diagnosis of Suspected PE

Symptoms & Signs

Symptoms and signs are non-specific. They can include breathlessness, chest pain, haemoptysis, and pre-syncope/syncope. Syncope is an important symptom as it may indicate RV dysfunction.

Initial Assessment

  • Assess medical history and perform physical examination.
  • Check for haemodynamic instability.
  • Perform ECG and CXR (can be normal or non-specific).
  • Consider the PERC rule to rule out PE in low-risk patients.
  • Use the 2-level PE Wells score to estimate clinical probability.

Management of High-Risk (Haemodynamically Unstable) PE

Defining Haemodynamic Instability

  • Cardiac Arrest: Need for CPR.
  • Obstructive Shock: SBP <90 mmHg (or vasopressors needed) despite adequate filling, with end-organ hypoperfusion.
  • Persistent Hypotension: SBP <90 mmHg or SBP drop ≥40 mmHg for >15 mins, not caused by arrhythmia, hypovolaemia, or sepsis.

Critically unwell patients: Document escalation plans and RESPECT forms. Senior input is mandatory.

Systemic Thrombolysis

Indicated for patients with confirmed high-risk PE (with haemodynamic instability). Do not use in haemodynamically stable patients.

Thrombolytic Agent Regime
Alteplase 10mg IV over 2 minutes, followed by 90mg IV over 2 hours.
(Total dose not to exceed 1.5mg/kg in patients <65kg).
In cardiac arrest: 50mg IV bolus STAT.
Streptokinase (accelerated) 1.5 million Units over 2 hours.

Contraindications

Absolute: Haemorrhagic stroke history, ischaemic stroke in last 6 months, CNS neoplasm, major trauma/surgery/head injury in last 3 weeks, active bleeding.

Relative: TIA in last 6 months, oral anticoagulant use, pregnancy, refractory hypertension (>180 mmHg systolic), active peptic ulcer. Weigh risk vs benefit.

Outpatient Management for Low-Risk PE

Low-risk patients (sPESI = 0) may be suitable for outpatient management. A careful risk assessment is required.

Interpretation:

  • Score = 0: Low Risk. Consider for outpatient management if no exclusion criteria are met.
  • Score ≥ 1: High Risk. Not suitable for outpatient management.

Exclusion Criteria for Outpatient Management

  • Haemodynamic instability or syncope/pre-syncope.
  • Evidence of RV dysfunction (ECG, CT, Echo, biomarkers).
  • Oxygen saturations <94% or oxygen requirement.
  • Active bleeding or high risk of major bleeding.
  • PE occurred while on full-dose anticoagulation.
  • Severe pain requiring opiates.
  • Other medical comorbidities requiring admission.
  • Severe renal disease (CrCl < 15ml/min) or severe liver disease.
  • Social reasons (inability to return home, compliance concerns, frailty).

Initial Anticoagulation

Start anticoagulation immediately in suspected PE if imaging is delayed. Choose an agent that can be continued if PE is confirmed. The choice depends on comorbidities and patient factors.

Patient Group Recommended Anticoagulation
Standard Patient (No major comorbidities) Apixaban or Rivaroxaban.
Apixaban: 10mg BD for 7 days, then 5mg BD.
If DOAC unsuitable LMWH for at least 5 days then Dabigatran or Edoxaban. Or LMWH/UFH with a VKA (Warfarin).
Renal Impairment (CrCl 15-50 ml/min) Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, or LMWH followed by Edoxaban. VKA is also an option.
Active Cancer Consider a DOAC (check for drug interactions). LMWH is a common alternative.
High-Risk PE (Haemodynamically unstable) IV Unfractionated Heparin (UFH) infusion.

Duration: Offer anticoagulation for at least 3 months. After this period, assess the risks and benefits of continuing treatment in the thrombosis clinic.
